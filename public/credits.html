The beginner loops use a local learned Markov rhythm model trained on 20 original authored patterns, with Rust synthesizing the voices. No neural or cloud music service is used. The named loops in the Loop builder (Lemon Haze, Low Tide and friends) come from the same model with fixed seeds; their instruments, sounds and mix were picked by hand. These generated loops are separate from the recorded-track library.

Millennium Chrome and Strawberry Mochi

Both built-in skins are original to bit.cheer: every sheet of them (main window, title bars, buttons, digits, lettering, sliders, equalizer and playlist frame) is drawn pixel by pixel by a script in this project. Skins you load from the Winamp Skin Museum are downloaded from the museum when you ask for them and belong to their creators.

Practice inspiration

Beatmatch PRO inspired genre-based track discovery. The catalog retains 574 public artist links from that practice library. Original audio stays with the artists on Bandcamp: open a source, obtain the track, then import your file into either deck. The 40 bundled Creative Commons excerpts above are a separate playable collection.

DJcompanion inspired training ideas including pitch sensitivity, cue timing, waveform study, ear practice, EQ, and local track loading. Bit Cheer's lesson progression and implementation are its own.

Make a skin of your own

Download the template in Skin studio, edit skin.toml, and import it again. Choose a structural base and add your own panel, titlebar, background, frame, transport buttons, number atlas, platter, and pitch-panel images. Classic WSZ imports retain the original bitmap controls. You can start with this Electric Blue TOML example.
