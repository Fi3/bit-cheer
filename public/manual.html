Chapter 01

Welcome to the booth

When a DJ blends one song into the next without a bump, the secret is beatmatching: making two tracks run at the same speed, with their beats landing at the same moment. Get it right and the two kick drums melt into one. Get it wrong and you hear a galloping "ba-dum, ba-dum" that slowly slides apart.

bit.cheer is a little school for exactly that skill. It gives you two decks, loops that start simple and get richer, real tracks for later, and a coach that scores how tight you are. The real teacher is your ears; the app just sets the table.

Beatmatching has two halves, and everything in this manual comes back to them:

  1. Tempo: both decks must play at the same speed. You fix that with the pitch fader.
  2. Phase: the beats must land together. You fix that with nudge.

What you need

  • A computer with a recent browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari). On a phone you get a retro "PC only" screen and a game of Snake instead. It's a whole bit. If you really insist, press OK, I'll get my PC five times and a secret button appears.
  • Headphones. Laptop speakers smear the beat; headphones make the drift easy to hear.
  • Five quiet minutes. That's enough for your first match.
Heads-up

Browsers only allow sound after you click something, so the audio engine wakes up with your first press of play. Until then the top bar says CLICK PLAY TO START; after that, ENGINE READY.

Chapter 03

Your first beatmatch

Start in 01 Foundation: two kick drums and nothing else. Deck A plays a kick called Velvet at 120 BPM, deck B a punchier one called Club Punch at 124 BPM. Different sounds, so you can tell them apart. Different speeds, so there's work to do.

  1. Press play on both. Click ▶ Play both decks at the top (or press Space). You'll hear two kicks, and they won't agree for long.
  2. Listen for the drift. Two kicks close together sound like a "flam": ba-dum. Listen for a few seconds. Does the gap grow? Shrink? Slide all the way round? That slide is the tempo difference. The BPM on each deck's screen shows it too: 120.00 versus 124.00.
  3. Match the tempo. Move deck B's pitch fader. Drag it, or put the mouse over it and roll the wheel for small, tidy steps. Up is faster, down is slower. Deck B is faster here, so bring it down until its BPM reads 120.00. Now the flam should stop sliding: whatever gap is left, it stays put.
  4. Line up the hits. Use deck B's NUDGE − and + buttons. Each press shifts the deck a tiny bit back or forward. Keep tapping until the two kicks fuse into one fat kick. If the flam gets wider, you're going the wrong way: tap the other button.
  5. Check your match. Let it play for a few seconds, then press Check my match in the coach (or Enter). You get a score out of 100. 85 or more is "That's the pocket."
  6. Go again. Press N for a fresh pair of kicks at new tempos. Feeling good? Click Ready for more? Build a layered loop → under the decks.
Tip

Do it with your eyes closed once the BPM is roughly right. The numbers get you close; your ears get you exact. That's the skill you're here for.

Which deck is ahead?

Hard to tell at first. Add the Monitor module (bottom of the page, + Add module…) and switch on Split cue: deck A goes to your left ear and deck B to your right. Suddenly "left first, then right" is obvious.

Chapter 04

The deck, control by control

Both decks have the same controls. Some are always there; others are modules you can add, fold away or remove (chapter 9).

The screen

  • The name of what's playing, with a badge: KICK-ONLY, LAYERED LOOP or TRACK.
  • BPM: the tempo you're actually hearing, pitch included. This is the number to match.
  • PITCH: how far the pitch fader is from zero, in percent.
  • SOURCE: the deck's own tempo at zero pitch.

The transport (always there)

ButtonWhat it does
▶ / ⅡPlay or pause this deck.
CUEStop and jump back to the cue point (the start, until you save another).
SETSave the current position as the cue point. Perfect for marking the first kick of a track.
NUDGE − / +Shift the deck a hair back or forward (0.04 of a beat). For lining up beats, not for speed.
Randomize tempoGive this deck a new tempo, a few BPM away from the other. Instant new challenge, one deck at a time.
Change trackNew material on this deck: another kick in Foundation, another named loop in the loop modes. (In Pro decks this lives in the track picker.)

The pitch fader

Your main tool. It speeds the deck up or slows it down; like a real turntable, the music's pitch moves with the tempo.

  • Drag it for big moves. Hold Shift while dragging for slow, precise moves.
  • Roll the mouse wheel over it: one small step per notch. Shift + wheel is the finest step there is, 0.01%.
  • − 0 + under it: 0.01% slower, back to zero, 0.01% faster.
  • Range (±8% to start): how far the fader can go. Pick ±2% for super-fine control or up to ±50% for big tempo gaps.
  • Wheel / keys: the size of one wheel or key step. Fine 0.01%, Normal 0.05%, Coarse 0.25%. The app remembers it for each deck.

Volume

One slider per deck, 0 to 100%. Deck volumes start at 80%.

Chapter 05

The five playgrounds

The mode bar on the left has five practice modes. They share the same desk; each one changes what's on the decks and what's hard. Clicking a mode always starts it fresh: the decks stop and new material loads.

01

Foundation

Two kicks. One groove.

Two kick drums and nothing else, so nothing distracts you from the drift. The decks always use two different kick sounds.

Try: N for new tempos until you can match in under a minute.

02

Loop builder

Layer up the challenge.

Loops with hats, snares, bass and friends. Build them yourself or load one of 24 named loops.

Try: add one element at a time and keep the match.

03

By ear

Trust what you hear.

The tempo numbers, waveforms and beat lights go dark. Just you and the sound.

Try: match first, then press B to peek at how close you got.

04

Star mode

Decks with trust issues.

Both decks start matched… then drift on their own, like real turntables. Hold the match for 60 seconds.

Try: tiny pitch moves the moment the kicks start to flam.

05

Pro decks

Real tracks. Real skills.

Forty real recordings in ten genres, plus your own music.

Try: mark the first kick with SET, start both on the beat with CUE and play.

01 · Foundation

The training wheels, and the place to come back to whenever things get muddy. The coach shows three steps (Listen, Match tempo, Nudge & check). Randomize tempo picks a new tempo close to the other deck, Change track swaps in a different kick sound (Velvet, Club Punch, 808 Boom, Dusty Tape, Knock or Pillow), and N deals a brand-new pair.

02 · Loop builder

Now there's more going on: a kick and a closed hat to start, and up to twenty instruments per deck. More layers make the beat harder to follow, which is exactly the point.

Named loops

Below each deck, the track picker holds twenty-four loops made by the app's own rhythm model, each with a name and a vibe: Lemon Haze (dusty lo-fi house), Low Tide (washed-out dub techno), Cloud Nine Café, Honey Drip, Night Bus Home and friends. Pick one in GENERATED LOOP and press Load (it loads paused, ready to start), or flip through them with ◀ Previous and Next ▶ while the music plays. Change track on the deck picks a random loop near the other deck's tempo.

The composer

Each deck's composer lists its instruments, one row each:

  • The name: click it to remove that instrument.
  • The sound: every instrument comes in named flavors. Chord can be Stab, Dreamy, Sunrise or Sus Haze; Noise can be Static, Vinyl, Ocean or Wind.
  • LEVEL and PAN: how loud, and where it sits from left to right.
  • SOLO: hear only this instrument (press again to bring the band back).
  • Sixteen steps: one bar. Click steps on and off to write your own pattern; the row switches from AUTO to MANUAL. Generate pattern hands it back to the rhythm model.

Add instruments with + Add instrument… (choose one) or + Add next (the next in line). Under the decks, + Add an element to both decks (=) grows both loops at once. And when you're feeling brave: CHAOS! throws a dense 12-to-20-instrument arrangement onto that deck, tempo untouched. Good luck in there.

03 · By ear

Like the Loop builder, but the tempo readouts show ---.--, the waveforms fade out ("LISTEN FOR THE DRIFT") and the beat lights switch off. The Hide BPM switch is on; flip it (or press B) to peek at the numbers, then hide them again. Use small pitch moves and listen to which way the drift goes: if it gets worse, reverse.

04 · Star mode

The endurance round. Both decks start at the same tempo, then each one wanders off speed by itself, up to 1% either way, changing its mind every few seconds. The sneaky part: the BPM screen and the pitch fader don't show the drift. Only your ears know.

  • The 60-second clock only runs while both decks play.
  • The coach shows LOCKED IN or DRIFTING…, the seconds left, and how long you've stayed in sync.
  • Finish the run ends it early (after 10 seconds); New run starts over with fresh loops.
Tip

Hold a nudge key instead of tapping it: holding K or L (deck B) leans on the deck like a hand on a vinyl platter, slowing it down or speeding it up until you let go.

05 · Pro decks

Real music. Two practice recordings are already loaded when you arrive. Each deck has its own track picker below the decks:

  1. Pick a genre in GENRE (House, Techno, Trance, Drum & Bass, Breakbeat, Electro, Dubstep, Hip Hop, Downtempo, Hardcore, or all of them).
  2. Pick a track in PLAYABLE TRACK. Titles only, no BPM: no peeking.
  3. Press Load. The track loads paused, at the start, at zero pitch. Or flip through with ◀ Previous, Next ▶ and Change track (random), which keep the deck playing.

Bring your own music

  • ↥ Import loads one audio file from your computer (up to 100 MB).
  • 📁 Browse folder opens your music folder right inside the picker. Choose the folder once (the dialog greys out the files inside; that's normal, you're choosing a folder), then wander its subfolders, type in Filter this folder, and click any track to load it. Hi-res FLAC is welcome, up to 300 MB.

Your files are never uploaded anywhere. They join this session's library under Local files, for both decks, until you reload the page.

Help the app understand your track

The app estimates a track's BPM, and estimates can be off (sometimes by exactly half or double). If you know better, type it in SOURCE BPM. Then play to the first big kick of a bar and press SET GRID: that tells the scoring where "beat one" is.

★ Star run

Want Star mode's drift on real tracks? Press ★ Star run at the top. ★ Stop star run cancels; ★ New star run goes again.

Find more music

The External track discovery module (add it from + Add module… at the bottom) links to 574 tracks from the original Beatmatch PRO catalog on their artists' pages. Get a track from its artist, then import it.

Chapter 06

How scoring works

Check my match looks at the last few seconds of both decks playing together, so a lucky moment doesn't count; a steady match does. Before it scores, it needs:

  • both decks playing, together, for at least 3 seconds since your last change (pitch, nudge, cue, new track…);
  • at least one instrument on each deck;
  • in Pro decks, a real track on both decks.

If something's missing, the notice bar tells you what. The score starts at 100 and loses points for two things:

  • Tempo gap: 16 points for every BPM the decks are apart.
  • Beat gap: a quarter of a point for every millisecond the kicks are apart, on average.

So a perfect tempo with kicks 20 ms apart scores about 95; half a BPM off with perfect kicks scores 92. 85 and up is the pocket and completes a lesson. The coach adds a hint (adjust the pitch, or nudge the late deck) and the exact gaps.

Every score lands in your history (the last 50 stay on this computer). You'll find your average and personal best on the Training page.

Heads-up

Scores measure tempo and timing, not taste. A score can't tell you whether two songs sound good together. Your ears are still the boss.

Chapter 07

The learning path

Not sure where to go next? Open Learning path in the top bar. Eight short lessons take you from two kicks to a real transition. Each one sets up the decks for you and puts its instructions in the coach. Score 85 or more to complete it and unlock the next.

#LessonWhat you practice
1Meet the beatTwo clean kicks: match the tempo, nudge them together.
2Catch the driftHear which deck is faster; close the gap in tiny steps.
3Find the pocketKick and hat: nudge until two kicks sound like one.
4Build the grooveHats, snare and percussion, without losing the beat.
5Under the surfaceA full loop with bass and melody; add an Equalizer to dig out the kick.
6Eyes closed, ears openThe BPM is hidden. Listen, adjust, nudge.
7Into the real worldTwo real tracks: find the first kicks, save cue points, match.
8Own the transitionHidden BPM, real tracks, and a crossfader handover.

Click Start lesson on a card (or Practice again on one you've finished). Locked cards say which lesson to finish first.

The lesson 8 handover

Lesson 8 wants a real DJ move: while both decks play matched, slide the crossfader from one end all the way to the other, taking at least 3 seconds, then check. The easy way: hold Shift + H (toward deck B) and the crossfader glides end to end in 6 smooth seconds.

Chapter 09

Make the desk yours

Modules: fold, remove, add

Almost everything on the desk is a module with two little buttons on its title bar:

  • Hide folds it down to its title; Open unfolds it.
  • × removes it.
  • + Add module… brings modules back, or adds new ones: at the foot of each deck for that deck, and after the shared modules for the shared ones (Crossfader, Master volume, Monitor, Audio outputs, Coach, Tap tempo, External track discovery).

Folding or removing a module never stops the music or forgets its settings. Each mode keeps its own arrangement, and the app remembers it next time. Only the transport (play, cue, nudge…) stays put, so you can never lose the play button.

Focus session

Want nothing but the essentials? Focus session ⛶ (or `) goes full screen with just each deck's ▶ Play, Ⅱ Pause, pitch fader and volume. Leave with Exit focus ✕ or Esc; everything is exactly where you left it, music included.

Hide BPM

The Hide BPM switch at the top hides the tempo numbers in any mode. Great for weaning yourself off the screen.

Tap tempo and the microphone

The Tap tempo module guesses a tempo from your taps: press TAP (or T) along with the kick. ♩ Listen with microphone estimates the tempo of music playing in your room (say, a record on your own turntable). The browser asks permission first; the sound is analysed on your computer and never recorded or sent anywhere.

Three rules that explain everything

  • Left is less, right is more. In every row, the key further left goes down, back or slower. The outer pitch keys (Q R, U P) move ten times as far as the inner ones.
  • Shift is finer. Hold Shift and every move becomes tiny (0.01% pitch). On the cue keys, Shift saves the cue instead (like SET).
  • Hold to keep going. Pitch and volume keys repeat; nudge keys held longer than a quarter second bend the deck's speed until you let go; G and H glide the crossfader.

More: V / M randomize a deck's tempo (with Shift: change track; in Pro decks they step to the next track), = adds an element to both decks, ` toggles Focus session, 1–5 switch modes, and Esc closes things. Keys work by position, so the shapes stay the same on AZERTY, QWERTZ and Italian keyboards.

Tip

Press ? any time for the keyboard help. It opens on the essentials; Show more reveals the full map with the real step sizes. Each key also makes its on-screen button glow, so you learn the map just by playing.

Chapter 11

Skins: dress up your decks

bit.cheer wears classic Winamp skins, the same ones people made by the thousand around the year 2000. Open Skin studio in the top bar. A skin changes how everything looks: buttons, sliders, digits, lettering, the deck windows. It never changes how anything works.

Two built-in skins

  • Millennium Chrome, the default: brushed chrome, cobalt screens and a lime glow.
  • Strawberry Mochi, the cute one: strawberry milk, mint, little hearts and a mochi friend in the corner.

Click a card to wear it. Download it for Winamp or Webamp ↓ gives you the skin file to use in a real Winamp, too.

Borrow any skin from the Winamp Skin Museum

  1. Open the museum. Go to skins.webamp.org and click a skin you like.
  2. Copy the link from your browser's address bar.
  3. Paste it in Skin studio and press Load skin (or Enter). Not sure? Use this example fills in a cute one, and Or this one a sci-fi tricorder.

↺ Back to Millennium Chrome takes you home whenever you like.

Pick a look

Old skins can be loud. Under LOOK, choose Original pixels (exactly as drawn), Comfort (gentler colors and textures) or Comfort + HD (the default: gentler, and redrawn four times sharper with smooth lettering).

Make your own

Press ↓ Download template, change the colors in skin.toml (and add your own images if you like), zip it up and press ↥ Import a skin file. You can import a .wsz Winamp skin you already have the same way. The skin guide walks you through it.

Chapter 12

Mixers and audio interfaces

Most people never need this chapter. It's for when you have a multichannel sound card and want each deck on its own channel, so you can mix them on a real DJ mixer.

  1. Add the Audio outputs module from + Add module… after the shared modules. It shows how many output channels your browser can reach.
  2. Pick an OUTPUT MODE. Stereo mix is the normal setting. Separate mono channels sends each deck to one channel (needs 2 or more). Separate stereo pairs gives each deck its own pair, such as 1–2 and 3–4 (needs 4 or more).
  3. Choose the channels for deck A and deck B, or press Swap A / B.

With separate outputs, the crossfader and split cue step aside (your mixer does that job now); deck volume, EQ and master volume keep working. If your interface doesn't show up, choose it in your computer's sound settings (or with Choose an output device… where the browser offers it) and press Refresh outputs. Browsers sometimes expose fewer channels than your interface has; the app shows you what it really gets.

Chapter 13

When something feels off

I don't hear anything.
  • Press play: sound starts with your first click (the top bar then says ENGINE READY).
  • Check the deck Volume, the Master volume and the crossfader: all the way to one side, only that deck plays.
  • In the Loop builder, a deck with no instruments is silent. Is a SOLO on?
  • Using Audio outputs? Make sure your mixer or interface channels are up.
Check my match won't give me a score.

Read the notice bar: it says what's missing. Usually both decks need to play together for 3 seconds after your last change. Any pitch move, nudge, cue or new track restarts that count, so let it settle, then check.

It sounds matched, but my score is low.
  • With real tracks, the BPM estimate may be off, sometimes by half or double. Fix it in SOURCE BPM.
  • Mark beat one with SET GRID on each track, so the scoring knows where the bar starts.
  • In the Find the one drill, the kicks can agree while the phrases don't: line up beat one of the 16-beat phrase.
I can't tell which deck is ahead.

Add the Monitor module and turn on Split cue: deck A in your left ear, deck B in your right. Or turn deck A's volume down for a moment, listen to deck B alone, then bring A back.

The keys stopped working.

A text field or dropdown probably has the keyboard (they keep their keys so you can type). Press Esc to let go of it. Shortcuts with Ctrl, Alt or Cmd always belong to the browser.

The folder dialog greys out all my files.

That's normal: it's asking for a folder, not a file. Pick the folder that holds your music (like Music) and confirm. Your tracks then appear inside the picker. If the browser asks to "upload" the files, they still stay on your computer.

My imported tracks disappeared.

Imported tracks and the chosen folder last for the current visit only. After a reload, import them again (with a folder, it's one click).

A museum skin won't load.

Copy the full address of the skin's page on skins.webamp.org (it ends in .wsz). Only classic Winamp 2 skins work; modern Winamp 3/5 skins don't.

I'm on a phone and only get Snake.

bit.cheer needs a computer: two decks and a crossfader don't fit next to your thumbs. Enjoy the Snake! (Press OK, I'll get my PC five times if you want to try anyway.)

Chapter 14

What stays on your computer

bit.cheer has no account and no server. Everything happens in your browser.

  • Remembered on this computer: your skin and look, how you arranged each mode, each deck's wheel step, the Winamp window settings, completed lessons and your last 50 scores.
  • For this visit only: imported tracks and the music folder you opened.
  • Never leaves your computer: your music and the microphone's sound. Nothing is uploaded.

Clearing your browser's site data resets the app to a fresh start.

Chapter 15

Little DJ dictionary

BPM
Beats per minute: how fast the music goes.
Pitch
How much faster or slower than normal a deck plays, in percent. On a turntable, the tempo and the musical pitch move together, and it's the same here.
Drift
Two beats sliding apart because the tempos don't quite match.
Flam
Two hits almost together, "ba-dum". The sound of a match that's close but not there.
Phase
Whether the beats land at the same moment. Same tempo, wrong phase: steady flam.
Nudge
A tiny push forward or back to line up the beats, without changing the tempo.
Cue point
A saved spot in a track, usually its first kick. CUE jumps back to it.
Downbeat / beat one
The first beat of a bar, where the music "lands".
Phrase
A bigger chunk of music, here 16 beats (four bars). Tracks change things at phrase boundaries.
Crossfader
The slider that blends from deck A to deck B.
Split cue
Deck A in one ear, deck B in the other, to hear each one clearly.
EQ
Equalizer: turn the lows, mids or highs of a deck up or down.
Skin
A costume for the app, in the Winamp tradition.
